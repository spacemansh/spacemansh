# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

spaceman.sh is for engineers, designers, open-source maintainers, technical founders, and software teams who build user interfaces. The audience spans beginners through senior practitioners, and they will most often meet the brand in developer contexts: GitHub, npm, docs, landing pages, READMEs, social profiles, CLI output, package icons, and product UI.

## Product Purpose

spaceman.sh builds open-source libraries, CLI tools, and UI tools that help people ship beautiful user interfaces without rebuilding every underlying layer from scratch. The brand exists to make software creation easier through developer tooling that feels considered, practical, and visually disciplined. Success means the tools feel easy to trust, easy to adopt, and worth using in public-facing work.

## Brand Personality

Minimal, technical, calm. The brand should feel modern and developer-focused without becoming cold, corporate, or theatrically futuristic. It should sit comfortably near respected developer-tool brands such as Vercel, shadcn/ui, and open-code style products, while keeping its own quiet point of view.

## Anti-references

Do not treat the current Suron landing page copy, logo, or product promise as spaceman.sh. Suron is a reference image for mood and landing-page feel only.

Avoid AI cliche, over-corporate SaaS identity, crypto styling, gaming marks, random startup templates, and obvious developer-logo shorthand. Specifically avoid angle brackets, terminal-window marks, hexagons, rocket icons, AI sparkles, gradients as identity, mascots, and any logo work that ignores the existing logo already in place.

The landing page should not look like a generic AI-generated developer tool site. It must support both light and dark modes.

## Design Principles

1. **Monochrome earns trust.** The identity is black-and-white first, with contrast, spacing, and rhythm carrying the brand before color does.
2. **Developer-native, not developer-costumed.** The work should feel familiar in GitHub, npm, docs, and CLI contexts without relying on code-window tropes.
3. **Beauty is part of the tool.** spaceman.sh promises useful UI tooling, so the interface itself must demonstrate taste, clarity, and restraint.
4. **Own the quiet.** Calm does not mean generic. Use deliberate composition, precise copy, and sharp interaction details to avoid template sameness.
5. **Reference mood, not content.** The reference images informs atmosphere and landing-page pacing only; spaceman.sh needs its own message, audience, and visual language.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for all text and controls in both light and dark modes. Preserve visible keyboard focus states, semantic form labels, reduced-motion alternatives, responsive layouts, and color-blind-safe interaction states. Do not rely on color alone to communicate state or hierarchy.
